A. Descriptive stats — review Example 1 & sample-exam lakes

Review: n = 20 ages, Σx = 143, Sxx = 62.55

Sorted: 4, 5, 5, 5, 6, 6, 6, 6, 6, 7, 7, 8, 8, 8, 9, 9, 9, 9, 10, 10

  1. Mean μ̂ = 143/20 = 7.15
  2. n even → median = average of 10th and 11th = (7+7)/2 = 7
  3. Lower half = first 10 values. Q1 = median of those = average of 5th and 6th of the lower half = (6+6)/2 = 6
  4. s² = Sxx/(n−1) = 62.55/19 = 3.292 → s = 1.81 (the review quotes Var = 3.29)
  5. Histogram: √20 ≈ 4.5 → use 5 bins. Width = (10−4)/5 = 1.2. Bins [4, 5.2], (5.2, 6.4], (6.4, 7.6], (7.6, 8.8], (8.8, 10] with counts 4, 5, 2, 3, 6.

Sample exam: mercury lakes 70, 85, 95, 80, 92, 97, 90, 100, 93, 95. Σx=897, Σx²=81,197

  1. Sorted: 70, 80, 85, 90, 92, 93, 95, 95, 97, 100
  2. Mean = 897/10 = 89.7
  3. 10% trimmed mean: 10% of 10 = 1 from each end. Drop 70 and 100. Remaining sum = 897−170 = 727. Mean = 727/8 = 90.875
  4. Median = average of 5th and 6th = (92+93)/2 = 92.5
  5. Sxx = 81197 − 897²/10 = 81197 − 80460.9 = 736.1. s² = 736.1/9 = 81.789. s = 9.044
On the exam: if they give Σx and Σx², use them. Show Sxx then divide by n−1. For trimmed mean, state how many you dropped.

B. Counting — words, rebar, students

Order matters, no repeats

5-letter words from 26 letters, no repeats: P(26,5) = 26×25×24×23×22 = 7,893,600

Order matters, repeats allowed

4-letter words, repeats OK: 264 = 456,976

Multivariate hypergeometric (sample exam rebar)

15 rebars: 5 of 1″, 5 of ¾″, 5 of ½″. Pull 6. P(two of each):

P = [C(5,2) C(5,2) C(5,2)] / C(15,6) = (10×10×10) / 5005 = 1000/5005 = 0.1998

Review Example 5 — 15 flu students, pick 3

4 seniors, 5 juniors, 6 freshmen. C(15,3)=455.

  • Exactly 2 freshmen: C(6,2)C(9,1)/455 = 135/455 = 0.2967
  • All 3 same category: [C(4,3)+C(5,3)+C(6,3)]/455 = (4+10+20)/455 = 34/455 = 0.0747
  • One of each: 4×5×6 / 455 = 120/455 = 0.2637
If the objects come in types and you sample without replacement, the fraction of combination products is almost always the answer. Write C(total, n) in the denominator first so you don’t forget it.

Sample exam: P(upgraded | stable)

P(U)=0.20, P(S|U)=0.90. From the filled table, 450 of 525 dams are stable, so P(S)=450/525=6/7.

P(U|S) = (0.90)(0.20) / (450/525) = 0.18 × 525/450 = 0.21 = 0.21
Always say whether 0.21 is a probability (unitless). Box it. If they already gave you a table, use the table’s P(S) rather than making up P(S|not upgraded).

D. Independence of two RVs — review Example 3

Marginals of S and T both look like (0.25, 0.5, 0.25). That does not mean independence.

Test: P(S=0)P(T=0) = 0.25×0.5 = 0.125, but the joint cell P(S=0, T=0) = 0.25 ≠ 0.125.

Dependent.

You only need one failing cell. A faster visual: several joint cells are 0 while both corresponding marginals are positive — impossible for independent RVs.

F. E and SD of a function of a listed pmf (sample exam high-rises)

HB takes 3, 5, 7 with probabilities 0.35, 0.45, 0.20. Cost C = h(HB) (whatever quadratic/linear they print).

  1. Make a three-row table: hb, p, c=h(hb), c².
  2. E[C] = Σ c p. Sample answer was $806.17 million.
  3. E[C²] = Σ c² p, then SD = √(E[C²]−(E[C])²). Sample answer $167.6 million.
  4. Because h is almost certainly not linear, do not compute h(E[HB]). E[HB]=4.7, and h(4.7) is the wrong expected cost.

G. Contingency table algebra (sample exam dams)

Urban given: Old-S 100, Old-U 30, New-S 200, New-U 20. Urban total = 350.

  • 1/3 of all dams rural ⇒ urban is 2/3 ⇒ Total = 350×3/2 = 525. Rural total = 175.
  • 50 older dams are unstable. Urban old-unstable = 30 ⇒ rural old-unstable = 20.
  • 1/6 of older rural dams are unstable ⇒ rural old total = 20×6 = 120 ⇒ rural old-stable = 100.
  • 1/3 of all stable dams are rural. Urban stable = 300 ⇒ all stable = 450 ⇒ rural stable = 150 ⇒ rural new-stable = 50.
  • Rural new total = 175−120=55 ⇒ rural new-unstable = 5.

Rural row: 100, 20, 50, 5. P(unstable | rural) = 25/175 = 0.1429.

Exactly 5 of 8 randomly selected older dams stable:

  • Older: 200 stable + 50 unstable = 250.
  • Exact hypergeometric: C(200,5)C(50,3)/C(250,8) ≈ 0.1471
  • Binomial approx p=0.8: C(8,5)(0.8)⁵(0.2)³ = 56×0.32768×0.008 = 0.1468
On Kockelman’s exam, either answer is typically accepted if you name the distribution. The binomial is faster on a calculator. Mention “n ≪ N so p≈constant” if you approximate.

H. Estimating n and p (sample exam EPA lakes)

Treat yearly mercury-lake count as X ~ Bin(n,p). Method of moments:

x̄ = 89.7 ≈ np
s² = 81.789 ≈ np(1−p)
1−p ≈ s²/x̄ = 81.789/89.7 = 0.91181 → p̂ = 0.08819
n̂ = 89.7 / 0.08819 ≈ 1017

Extra credit — why binomial may be wrong: contamination is spatially clustered (trials not independent); the set of U.S. lakes is finite (should be hypergeometric); p can trend over 10 years; EPA may not use simple random sampling; n may not actually be constant.

I. What to write in the first 60 seconds

  1. cdf = cumulative distribution function
  2. Bayes: P(Ai|B) = P(B|Ai)P(Ai) / Σj P(B|Aj)P(Aj)
  3. E[h(X)]=h(E[X]) iff h(x)=a+bx (linear)
  4. Dump the formula block from the cheat sheet onto scrap paper in case you cannot bring this sheet in.
